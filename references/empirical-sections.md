# Natural writing in empirical sections

## Data and measurement

Introduce each dataset because it answers a question. State the observation, population, date, geographic unit, and relevant exclusion before technical construction becomes dense. Keep the measured object separate from the concept it may proxy. An administrative status can be a basis for public commemoration without measuring what anyone remembered.

Put formulas where they clarify the definition. Explain the unit in words. If an outcome is a percentage, percentage-point coefficients and percent changes are not interchangeable. IHS coefficients are not automatically elasticities or percentages; a transformed count and a transformed population rate measure different objects. Preserve missing values and source coverage in the interpretation.

Keep replication downloads and implementation records outside publication prose unless the user requests them or the journal requires a data statement. Cite the underlying article or dataset appropriately. A table note should explain the measurement used, not narrate file processing.

## Empirical comparison and identification

A readable explanation can begin with two groups of people or places. Explain what differs, what is held common, and which change the design uses. Name the estimator after the comparison is intelligible. This does not mean every design has two binary treatment groups: describe continuous intensity and the source of identifying variation accurately.

Introduce fixed effects through the differences they absorb. County FE remove permanent county differences; birth-year FE absorb common birth-cohort differences. They do not by themselves remove differential cohort trends. If an event study has a wartime reference, name that reference before interpreting negative event times as untreated periods.

Motivate an identifying comparison through the economic problem and the source of variation. When a check addresses an alternative story, name that story and explain what the check can distinguish. This is not a requirement to open every design paragraph with a concern. Avoid saying that a long control list “rules out” every alternative. A strong first stage establishes relevance, not exclusion. A joint weak-IV-robust test is not a test of one selected coefficient.

For an IV–OLS gap, establish common samples and units before explaining why the estimators can differ. Measurement error and heterogeneous responses can motivate a larger IV coefficient when the cited theory and the instrument fit. Distinguish these explanations from independent evidence that one of them generated the observed gap. Give the economic explanation before its specific qualification; neither an unusually large estimate nor the existence of identifying assumptions is by itself a reason to describe the analysis as defective. Keep a concrete exclusion condition with the interpretation and preserve evidence that bears on it.

## Results

A useful paragraph has an economic question, a comparison, evidence, and an implication. This is a diagnostic, not a four-sentence template. Sometimes one sentence does two jobs; sometimes measurement needs more explanation first.

Select numbers that let the reader assess the point. Identify the unit and reference group before interpreting a magnitude. Use a verified outcome mean, actual policy change, or other relevant benchmark. Do not invent an IQR because other papers use one. Keep coefficient differences on compatible samples and scales. If several outcomes tell different stories, explain the distinction instead of calling the whole result “robust.”

Check the declared core outcomes before shortening a results summary. A concise version should not silently narrow the paper's main outcome set. “Statistically significant” or “significantly larger” is appropriate when supported by the reported inference; make the relevant comparison clear and do not use significance as a substitute for magnitude or economic meaning.

Integrate tables and figures into the argument. “Table X shows that ...” should lead to a substantive finding. Identify a panel's parent table or figure when first discussing it, after a subsection boundary, and after switching exhibits. Bare panel letters are useful only while the parent exhibit remains unambiguous. Avoid an inventory paragraph in which each sentence merely announces another table. In the introduction, summarize the substantive result rather than announcing where appendix estimates, sample definitions, or diagnostics are filed. Retain an appendix reference there only when it is needed to assess the argument.

An estimate close to zero with a wide interval is inconclusive, not evidence of no effect. A positive estimate that becomes much smaller is sensitive in magnitude even if still significant. A change in sign in a restricted sample can arise before adding a control. Say which change occurred.

## Robustness and heterogeneity

Begin with the reason for the check. If the historical variable reduces coverage, compare baseline and controlled specifications on the same available sample. Separate the effect of restriction from the effect of adjustment. Describe a post-treatment control as sensitivity to another account, not as a predetermined confounder by default.

For heterogeneity, distinguish the subgroup coefficient from the difference between subgroups. Statistical significance in one group and insignificance in another does not establish a difference. Explain the interaction test and its group definition. Do not call a subgroup institution a mechanism when it only describes a context.

Avoid choosing specifications or removing findings to obtain significance. User-authorized consolidation or removal of an exhibit can improve focus, but material sensitivity that changes the paper's conclusion should remain clear in the relevant discussion.

## Mechanisms and further analysis

Let the proposed behavioral link motivate each source. Distinguish an institution's presence, its construction or naming date, publicity, participation, attitudes, and skills. Do not turn these different observations into an observed causal sequence. A later attitude survey can describe a present association without recovering beliefs during a historical program.

For survey batteries, check the direction and object of each item. Agreement with a cost barrier and agreement with equal access do not have the same interpretation. A belief about parental education as a source of success is not willingness to invest in a child.

Keep direct public action, indirect attention, and individual responses distinct when those are the user's chosen mechanism categories. Mixed evidence should change the mechanism claim. Explain an important null or negative result once where it bears on that claim, rather than adding the same defensive sentence after every estimate.

For intergenerational work, name whose cohort determines exposure, whose outcome is measured, which relatives are linked, and what co-residence selects. A household with grandchildren is not automatically a sample of parents. Current residence is not childhood residence. A selected spending outcome does not isolate preference transmission.

## Notes and appendix

Make notes understandable without the conversation. Define dependent variables, outcome scales, sample differences, exposure units, reference categories, key included terms, fixed effects, weighting, and inference as relevant. Do not repeat the full methods section mechanically. Keep table labels short and use notes for definitions.

Apply one consistent set of outcomes to tables addressing the same education question, unless a source genuinely lacks an outcome. Distinguish unavailable data from an omitted presentation. Consolidate closely related panels when feasible, preserving readable type and all numerical results. Place each further analysis coherently, and cite appendix material where it advances the main argument.
